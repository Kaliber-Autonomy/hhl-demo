Who we are

A freight forwarder and customs broker working a single trade lane: Atlantic Canada to the Netherlands, and back again.

The company

We only work one route

A large carrier treats this corridor as a feeder into a hub somewhere else. Boxes get transhipped, schedules slip, and the person on the phone has never seen your container.

Our answer was to pick one lane and put a named coordinator on every account. That does limit us: if you need cargo moved to Singapore we are the wrong company. The trade-off is that when you call about a shipment, whoever picks up already knows which ship it is on and where the customs file has got to.

Customs sits inside the business rather than being contracted out, because that is the part of a Canada to Europe movement most likely to cost you money. Anyone can arrange a container. Fewer people will tell you, before you book, that your goods will not qualify for the preferential rate you were counting on.

Nova Scotia roots

Why Halifax

It is the closest North American container port to Europe, it stays ice free through the winter, and the rail runs onto the dock. The Port of Halifax puts rail transit to Chicago, Detroit and St Paul at four to seven days. Those are the reasons, and they are checkable.

Dutch partnership

Local hands at the European end

Our Rotterdam partner holds the Dutch licences, including the fiscal representation that lets non-resident importers defer VAT under Article 23. Customs, terminal relationships and onward transport are theirs to run, in their own time zone.
