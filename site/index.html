Why this route

Halifax is the closest North American port to Europe

That is why transatlantic services treat it as the first port inbound and the last port outbound. Cargo loaded here is at sea sooner than cargo trucked south to a bigger hub.

The sea distance to Rotterdam is 3,854 nautical miles, which comparable services cover in about eleven days. At the far end, Rotterdam sits in front of a hinterland of roughly 500 million people, reachable by the Betuwe freight line and by Rhine barge as far as Basel.

Rail runs straight off the dock at Halifax, so inbound European cargo can be in Chicago, Detroit or St Paul within four to seven days of discharge.

Details

Tariffs

Under CETA, most of this cargo crosses at zero duty

When the agreement came into force, 98 per cent of EU tariff lines went duty free for Canadian goods, and another one per cent was phased out over the seven years that followed. On industrial goods the EU opened 99.4 per cent immediately and Canada 99.6 per cent.

The rate is only available if the goods actually originate in Canada under the agreement's rules, which cap non-originating material at 50 per cent of the value and tighten that to 45 per cent in some sectors. You claim it with an origin declaration written on the invoice. Canadian law then expects you to keep the supporting records for six years.

Getting that wrong is expensive and it surfaces long after the container has been delivered. We would rather do the classification before you book.

Cargo on this lane

What actually moves between Nova Scotia and the Netherlands

Nova Scotia exports about $827 million of seafood a year, $449 million of it fresh lobster, and the European Union is one of its three largest seafood markets.

Canada to Netherlands

Seafood

Lobster, crab and groundfish. The paperwork is the hard part: the processing plant has to be on the EU approved list, and every shipment needs a CFIA health certificate raised through TRACES NT plus a catch certificate from Fisheries and Oceans.

Canada to Netherlands

Wood and paper

Nova Scotia ships around $219 million of chemical wood pulp and $229 million of paper products a year. Most of it consolidates well into full containers.

Canada to Netherlands

Berries and agri-food

Frozen wild blueberries, apples and prepared fruit, moving in reefer boxes.

Netherlands to Canada

Bulbs and nursery stock

Live plant material needs a phytosanitary certificate and often an inspection on arrival, so we pre-lodge the documents rather than letting the box sit at the terminal waiting on paperwork.

Netherlands to Canada

Machinery and parts

Production equipment and spares, including units too tall or too heavy for a standard box.

Netherlands to Canada

Food and consumer goods

Cheese, confectionery and packaged goods. Dairy is the awkward one, because it moves under tariff rate quotas instead of a flat preferential rate, so we check quota availability before quoting.

Why now

Canadian exporters are already moving volume away from the United States

The share of Canadian exports going to the United States fell ten percentage points to 68 per cent between May 2024 and May 2025. In one month this spring, merchandise exports to everywhere else rose 24.8 per cent, the second largest monthly increase on record, while exports south fell 6.6 per cent.

Some of that is a handful of commodities rather than a broad shift, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise. But the direction is real, and the Netherlands is showing up in the figures: Canadian aluminium exports rose 50.7 per cent in a single month to $1.2 billion, led by shipments to the Netherlands, Italy and Greece.

For a Nova Scotia exporter with a European buyer, the constraint has rarely been demand. It has been finding somebody who will handle both the crossing and the customs file without handing it off three times.
